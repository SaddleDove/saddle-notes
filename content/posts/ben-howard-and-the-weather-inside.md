Ben Howard makes music that sounds like weather. Not the dramatic weather — the kind that makes headlines — but the ordinary weather of a person: the low pressure system, the long grey afternoon, the strange calm that arrives after something ends.

*I Forget Where We Were* is the record I play when things are breaking and there's nothing to do but wait. It doesn't fix anything. That's the point.

<!--more-->

## The patience album

There's a quality to Howard's records that I can only describe as *patience held at full volume*. Nothing rushes. Guitars arrive late, leave early, and never apologise. The drums sound like a room, not a machine. Listening to it feels like sitting by a window during rain — you're not doing anything, and somehow that's the correct response to the weather.

## When the build breaks

I have a professional ritual now. When CI is red and the fix is unclear, I play *I Forget Where We Were* and stop trying to force the code to make sense. I read the error message like a line of poetry — what is it actually saying? What does it want?

Most bugs are communication failures. The code is trying to tell you something and you're too anxious to listen. Howard's music lowers the volume of my own panic until the message gets through.

## The chorus that isn't

There's a moment on *In Dreams* where the song seems to stop being a song and becomes just weather: a field, a wind, a voice deciding not to rise. I have never been able to describe it properly, which is why I keep listening. Some things don't want to be fixed or explained. They want to be witnessed.

I try to write code like that now. Not the code — the documentation. The spaces. The willingness to let a function be simple and quiet and done. Patience is a design principle. Ben Howard just happens to be its best textbook.
